What you are seeing

Every atom starts with the same velocity, in a strip at the left, a very special arrangement. Nothing much happens until the first atoms reach the right wall. Then collisions start and the beam dissolves into a gas.

Entropy here is a coarse-grained count of how spread out the atoms are: the box is divided into cells in position and in velocity, and S/N = −Σ p ln p over the cells, in units of kB. It is low when the atoms occupy few cells (all in one strip, all with one velocity) and high when they are spread over many.

Reverse time flips every velocity, v → −v. Because hard-disk collisions are perfectly time-symmetric, the atoms should then retrace their paths exactly, and the arrows show it. The lower plot is the simulation clock t, which now counts down. The entropy plot uses elapsed time, which never does.

Try this

  1. Let it run until the gas looks mixed (about 30 ps), then press Reverse time. What does the entropy do? What do the atoms do?
  2. When t gets back to 0 the simulation pauses. Are the atoms inside their dashed rings? How large is the error?
  3. Press Resume and keep going. What does the entropy do after t = 0? Is there anything in the equations that picks one direction as "forward"?
  4. Restart and let it run for 100 ps or more before reversing. Does t = 0 bring the atoms home now? Why not, if the dynamics is exactly reversible?
  5. Lower the numerical precision to 5 digits and repeat the experiment at 30 ps. Then try 10 and 12 digits. What decides how long reversal works?
  6. Try N = 20 and N = 100. Which is easier to reverse? Which has a smoother entropy curve, and why?
  7. The reversed state is a perfectly legal state of the gas. Why, in a real gas, do we never see it happen by itself?
About the model

Hard disks (diameter 0.34 nm, mass 39.9 u, like argon atoms) in a closed 2D box with hard walls, so there are no periodic boundaries. Collisions are elastic and are solved exactly (event-driven dynamics) in steps of 0.07 ps. The atoms start in a strip on the left, all with the same velocity, 1.5 v0 ≈ 375 m/s to the right. Time reversal simply sets v → −v for every atom.

The numerical precision slider rounds every coordinate and velocity to that many decimal places after each step. At the top setting (16) nothing extra is rounded and the computer's own 64-bit arithmetic (about 16 significant digits) is the only source of error. Each collision magnifies tiny differences in an atom's direction, so a rounding error of 10−16 grows by roughly a factor of 109 every 10 time units: reversal works beautifully at first, then fails abruptly. This sensitivity is the signature of chaos, and it is why a macroscopic gas never un-mixes in practice.

Entropy: 8 × 5 spatial cells and 1 v0-wide velocity bins for vx and vy (from about −5 to +5 v0); the plot shows the position part, the velocity part, and their sum. With few atoms the estimate is noisy and is capped near ln of the number of occupied cells. Links: ?n=40&digits=8&speed=2.
